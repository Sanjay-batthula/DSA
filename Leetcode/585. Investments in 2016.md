# 585. Investments in 2016

## Problem Statement

Return the sum of `tiv_2016`, rounded to two decimal places, for policies whose `tiv_2015` value is shared by at least one other policy and whose `(lat, lon)` location is unique.

The `Insurance` table has a unique `pid`. `lat` and `lon` are not `NULL`.

## Sample Test Case

```text
Insurance:
+-----+----------+----------+-----+-----+
| pid | tiv_2015 | tiv_2016 | lat | lon |
+-----+----------+----------+-----+-----+
| 1   | 10       | 5        | 10  | 10  |
| 2   | 20       | 20       | 20  | 20  |
| 3   | 10       | 30       | 20  | 20  |
| 4   | 10       | 40       | 40  | 40  |
+-----+----------+----------+-----+-----+
Output:
+----------+
| tiv_2016 |
+----------+
| 45.00    |
+----------+
```

Policies 1 and 4 have a repeated `tiv_2015` value and unique locations, so their 2016 investments sum to `45`.

## Grouped Subqueries with `IN` ⚠️ Potentially Incorrect

```sql
SELECT ROUND(SUM(tiv_2016), 2) AS tiv_2016
FROM Insurance
WHERE tiv_2015 IN (
    SELECT tiv_2015
    FROM Insurance
    GROUP BY tiv_2015
    HAVING COUNT(*) > 1
)
AND CONCAT(lat, lon) IN (
    SELECT CONCAT(lat, lon)
    FROM Insurance
    GROUP BY lat, lon
    HAVING COUNT(*) = 1
);
```

Concatenating `lat` and `lon` can make different coordinate pairs produce the same string, so this location check is not reliable for every input.

## Explanation

| Section | Details |
|---|---|
| **Intuition** | Keep policies satisfying both independent conditions, then sum their 2016 investment values. |
| **Approach** | The first subquery returns `tiv_2015` values occurring more than once. The second intends to return locations occurring exactly once, but concatenating coordinates can cause collisions. The outer query filters by both sets and rounds `SUM(tiv_2016)` to two decimal places. |
| **Key Concepts** | `GROUP BY`, `HAVING`, `IN`, `COUNT()`, `SUM()`, `ROUND()` |

## CTEs + Tuple Joins ✅

```sql
WITH tiv_count AS (
    SELECT tiv_2015
    FROM Insurance
    GROUP BY tiv_2015
    HAVING COUNT(*) > 1
),
loc_unique AS (
    SELECT lat, lon
    FROM Insurance
    GROUP BY lat, lon
    HAVING COUNT(*) = 1
)
SELECT ROUND(SUM(i.tiv_2016), 2) AS tiv_2016
FROM Insurance i
JOIN tiv_count t
    ON i.tiv_2015 = t.tiv_2015
JOIN loc_unique l
    ON i.lat = l.lat
   AND i.lon = l.lon;
```

Each CTE selects one qualifying set of values, and the joins retain policies meeting both conditions. Comparing `lat` and `lon` separately avoids the concatenation collision risk.

## Summary

- Require `tiv_2015` to occur more than once and `(lat, lon)` to occur exactly once.
- Apply both filters before summing `tiv_2016`.
- The concatenated-coordinate subquery can collide; the tuple-based CTE joins compare the location columns separately.
